# Topic Title

## 1. Overview

- **Timeline:** 6 months before departure
- **Goal:** Determine your future tax residency status in the new jurisdiction and understand the related responsibilities you will have while living abroad in respect to past country of residency.

## 2. Importance 

- **The Benefit:** (What do you gain by doing this right?)
    1. Knowing your future tax residency status will help you understand the taxes you are obligated to pay in your destination country.
    2. Understanding your responsibilities towards your past country of residence can prevent potential complications or penalties during tax audits.
- **The Risk:** (What is the penalty/consequence of failing or delaying? e.g., Fines, deportation, homelessness.)
    1. Neglecting to determine your future tax residency status could lead to underpayment or overpayment of taxes in either your destination country or your past country of residence.
    2. Ignoring your responsibilities towards your past country of residence may result in penalties or complications during tax audits.

## 3. Prerequisites 
*What you need to have ready before starting.*

- **Documents:**
	- None
- **Costs:** 
	- None
- **Dependencies:** 
	- [[Source of Funds and Budget]]
	- [[Centre of Vital Interests]]

## 4. Instructions 
*The step-by-step execution plan.*

1. **Step One:** Research the tax laws of your destination country to understand the criteria for becoming a tax resident.
2. **Step Two:** Review the duration of your stay, ties to the destination country, and other factors that may affect your tax residency status in your new location.
3. **Step Three:** If necessary, contact a tax advisor or legal expert in your destination country to clarify any uncertainties or obtain guidance on your specific situation.
    1. (Optional) Consult with a tax advisor or legal expert from your past country of residence regarding your responsibilities towards them while living abroad.
   
> [!TIP] Pro Tip
> Be aware that different countries have varying definitions of tax residency, so it is essential to research and consult experts in both your destination country and your past country of residence to ensure compliance.
## 5. Pitfalls
*Common mistakes and "anti-patterns" to avoid.*

> [!DANGER] Things to Avoid
> - **Mistake 1:** Failing to research the criteria for tax residency in your destination country could result in unintentional underpayment or overpayment of taxes.
> - **Mistake 2:** Neglecting to consult with a legal expert or tax advisor may lead to potential complications during tax audits or non-compliance with regulations in either your new location or past country of residence.
